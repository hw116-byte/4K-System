Pin map & connectors 1

FE-62Minor

The Frontend's two board-to-board sockets have no pin-1 mark, so it can be plugged onto the FPGA-LDO board turned half a turn, putting 3.3 V and 5 V on signal pins.

J1 and J2 (Hirose DF40C-70DS, 0.4 mm-pitch board-to-board receptacles on the bottom) have no silkscreen at all: their footprints carry only fabrication-layer outlines, their reference texts are hidden, and nothing else on the bottom marks them. The matching FPGA-LDO A headers do carry pin-1 arrows. The pair is symmetric under a 180 degree turn: turned, Frontend J1 pin 1 lands on FPGA-LDO J2 pin 70, so each pin n mates with pin 71-n and J1 and J2 swap; only 2 of the 140 contacts then carry the same net. Frontend VIN_3V3 (J2 pins 68-70) lands on FPGA-LDO J1 pins 3/2/1 (ASIC8_READ, BOARD3_CLK, ASIC6_DATA1), and VIN_5V (J1 pin 32) lands on ASIC3_DATA4; these FPGA-LDO lines go only to the ASIC sockets (and R21). The FPGA-LDO board's own 3.3 V contacts land on Frontend J1 pins 3/2/1, which are FPGA balls. Frontend ground lands on 13 FPGA-LDO signal contacts, and FPGA-LDO ground on 13 Frontend FPGA signal pins. In bring-up stage 3 of the user guide the Frontend is mated to the FPGA-LDO board with no ASIC boards fitted, so no ASIC stack is there to block a turned fit. The turned Frontend still lies inside the FPGA-LDO outline; whether its bottom parts would stop it seating fully was not checked. User guide section B2.2 warns only about the ASIC boards. The FPGA-LDO and ASIC boards carry pin-1 dots and outlines at their board-to-board pairs; the Frontend does not.

Where
Frontend J1 (122.245, 112.57) and J2 (104.745, 112.57), bottom; pin 1 at (120.705, 119.37) and (103.205, 119.37). Mates FPGA-LDO A J1 (91.255, 127.0) and J2 (108.755, 127.0), top. Turned case: Frontend J2.68-70 VIN_3V3 -> FPGA-LDO J1.3/2/1; Frontend J1.32 VIN_5V -> FPGA-LDO J2.39.
If left alone
A Frontend turned half a turn puts its 3.3 V and 5 V onto FPGA-LDO lines meant for 1.5 V ASIC signals and ties 13 FPGA signal pins to ground. It also leaves the FPGA-LDO board's 3.3 V fed only through three FPGA pins (U15, V17, V16), so its regulators try to start from FPGA pins. FPGA pins damaged this way could later harm the bonded ASICs once those are fitted.
What to do
Add a pin-1 dot and a connector outline on the bottom silkscreen at J1 and J2, matching the FPGA-LDO A marks (no copper change). Name the Frontend's orientation in user guide sections B2.2 and B5 (stage 3).
Source
Artix_Frontend_8L_V3.kicad_pcb (Frontend 14 layers V13): J1/J2 footprints, bottom silkscreen and pad nets; FPGA_LDO_A_Layout_V24.kicad_pcb: J1/J2 pin-1 marks and pad nets; system datasheet user guide B2.2 and B5; Hirose DF40 series, https://www.hirose.com/en/product/series/DF40

Grounding & return paths 4

FE-10Minor

Most ASIC lanes that change layer at J2 have no ground via stack nearby for their return current.

At J2, 40 lanes run on L8 or L10 and drop to L14 through a buried via and laser vias. Their return current must move from the inner ground planes (L7, L9, L11) to L13, which needs a ground via stack (L2 to L13) close by. The J2 field has only one such stack, at (106.0, 104.4). 36 of the 40 lanes are more than 2 mm and 34 more than 3 mm from the nearest stack; the median is 5.0 mm and the worst 6.84 mm. On the J1 side the same check gives at most 1.85 mm. The project's routing rule G05 asks for a nearby ground return transition at each signal via. Across the board there are 683 ground vias but only 55 full L2-L13 ground stacks (one of them runs L1-L14), and the only through-hole ground pads are J3's four shield slots. The layer changes of the cable LVDS pairs, the transceiver reference clock and the 25 MHz clock each have a ground stack 0.42-1.13 mm away, so the long return paths are limited to the J2 field and the bottom capacitors (FE-21).

Where
Frontend J2 field, x 102.2-107.5 mm, y 104.4-121.9 mm. Worst: /ASIC5_DATA4 (103.02, 117.4) L10, 6.84 mm; /ASIC5_DATA2 6.60 mm; BOARD3_SPI_CLK L8 6.49 mm; /ASIC5_SYNC 6.45 mm.
If left alone
The lanes, including the returned 32 MHz clocks and an SPI clock, share long return detours near the ASIC connector, which adds ground bounce and crosstalk. The lanes use slow slew, so a failure is unlikely, but a glitch cannot be ruled out without ASIC edge data.
What to do
Add full ground via stacks (buried via plus lasers to L2 and L13) beside the J2 GND pins and in the J2 via rows where room allows, then repeat the distance check aiming for about 1-1.5 mm, as on the J1 side.
Source
Frontend PCB file, via positions and spans (pcbnew); project routing ground rules V1, rule G05

Layout & manufacturing 8

FE-11Minor

JLC has not accepted in writing the laser vias stacked up to six high and the 0.170 mm hole-to-copper gaps.

The board has 2,774 vias and no through vias: 2,373 laser vias (0.10 mm hole, 0.25 mm land) and 401 buried L4-L11 vias (0.15/0.30 mm). 241 sites stack three laser vias on L1-L4 and 188 on L11-L14. Counting all laser vias stacked at one point, 226 sites have 3, 153 have 4, 23 have 5 and 29 have 6; the 29 six-high sites sit on a buried via and form full L1-L14 stacks. 606 laser vias sit directly on a buried via (276 on L3-L4, 330 on L11-L12); KiCad's co-located-hole warning lists only 71 of them, so DRC understates the stacking. JLC's HDI page supports stacked vias but gives no stack-height limit and prefers staggered vias. The laser hole and land, the 0.071 mm dielectric and the buried-via aspect ratio (about 6.4) are within JLC's HDI FAQ, and the closest holes of different nets are 0.247 mm apart against the project's 0.24 mm rule. Separately, at 199 places a via hole comes within 0.170-0.199 mm of copper of another net (DRC with a 0.20 mm hole-clearance rule: 199 violations on 182 vias and tracks, none below 0.170 mm, never next to a pour). JLC's HDI figure is 0.15 mm, but its general rigid-board table says 0.20 mm; the project rule is 0.15 mm, so the normal DRC does not flag these. In the 16 Nov cart the buried vias and laminations were 'manual quote', and the PCB remark box held only 178 characters of the order note. Eight questions on the HDI build, the stacked laser vias among them, went to JLC support by e-mail on 19 Nov 2026; there is no answer yet.

Where
Frontend U1 BGA field and the J1/J2 fan-out; tightest holes on inner layers (for example buried vias at x 115.02-115.82, y 109.40 on L4).
If left alone
JLC may re-quote, ask for changes or trim copper near holes during its engineering review, delaying the order. Stacks three to six high on capped buried vias are a known weak point for via reliability. Moving to staggered vias would mean re-routing the U1 field.
What to do
Before paying, get JLC's written acceptance of these stacks on capped buried vias and of 0.170 mm hole-to-copper. Its answer to the 19 Nov 2026 e-mail is the place for it; if that e-mail did not carry the via counts (stacks of 3-6 lasers, 29 full L1-L14 stacks, 606 lasers on buried vias) and the 0.170 mm figure, send them too.

FE-30Minor

J3's shield tabs get only a top fillet of solder, and a bottom-side touch-up could bridge VIN_3V3 to ground.

The four shield tabs of the Micro-HDMI receptacle J3 sit in plated slots and are soldered by paste printed on the top pads only (1.5 x 2.55 mm opening, about 0.17-0.20 mm3 of solder per slot against about 1.6 mm3 of slot volume). Molex draws the tabs 0.55 +/- 0.15 mm long, so on the 1.574 mm board they stop well short of the bottom. Each joint is therefore mainly a top fillet. The order note asks JLC to 'make sure all four are soldered', which could prompt a bottom touch-up; on the bottom the slot ring is 0.16 mm from C83 (VIN_3V3) and 0.18 mm from R27 (VCCAUX_1V8). The slot barrels are also tied solidly, with no thermal spokes, to all five inner ground planes (L2, L7, L9, L11, L13): every ground pour uses a solid pad connection and no design rule overrides it for J3. In reflow the whole board heats, so this barely changes the joints, but it makes hand touch-up or rework of J3 hard.

Where
Frontend J3 shield slots at (109.4, 98.28), (109.4, 101.64), (115.6, 98.28), (115.6, 101.64); bottom C83 pad 1 (VIN_3V3), R27 pad 1 (VCCAUX_1V8); ground pours on L2, L7, L9, L11, L13.
If left alone
The tab joints are J3's only anchor against cable forces; weak joints let plugging forces load the 0.4 mm-pitch signal joints. A bottom touch-up could short VIN_3V3 or VCCAUX_1V8 to ground. Reworking J3, the board's only cable connector, is difficult because the slots sink heat into five planes.
What to do
Change the assembly note to ask for top-side solder only on the shield tabs, with no bottom touch-up. Pull-test J3 on one assembled board before relying on it. On a later layout, give the J3 shield pads 2-4 thermal spokes on the inner ground layers.
Source
Frontend PCB file (J3 slot pads: F.Paste only; L14 gaps; ground pour pad connection and .kicad_dru rules); Frontend order note (assembly remark, J3 line); Molex SD-46765-001 drawing, https://www.lcsc.com/datasheet/C563878.pdf

FE-49Minor

JLC has not said how it reads bottom-side angles or library angle offsets; a wrong reading would land up to nine polarised bottom parts turned half a turn.

The placement file gives KiCad's own angle for bottom parts, and the order note says so and lists pin 1 for 22 parts. JLC publishes no bottom-side rule, while common tools (kicad-jlcpcb-tools, KiBot) write 180 minus the angle. Under that rule U5, U6, U10, U11, U14, Y1 and Y2 (CPL angle 0 or 180) would be turned 180 degrees. Their pads are symmetric, so they would solder down with supply and ground swapped. A second, separate risk: U8 (flash level shifter) and U15 (transceiver-enable translator) carry a +90 degree library offset in the placement file (U8 90 against KiCad's 0, U15 270 against 180; U11, U17 and U18 carry the same offset). The order note says the offsets are included but not how they apply to a mirrored bottom part; if JLC applies them with the opposite sign, U8 and U15 land turned 180 degrees. U8's GND (pin 8) would then sit on pad 16 (VCCO_1V5) and its VCCA (pin 14) on a GND pad; U15's GND (pin 2) would sit on pad 5 (VCCO_1V5). U17 and U18 are symmetric, so a half turn is harmless for them. The pin-1 table lists U8 and U15. Y1 and U7 have no silkscreen pin-1 mark; U12 has one short line. In the 16 Nov cart the bottom side 'looked aligned' in JLC's preview, but it was not checked part by part.

Where
Frontend bottom: U5 (115.005, 119.920), U6 (108.875, 119.920), U8 (113.780, 106.250), U10 (118.050, 99.150), U11 (111.900, 100.260), U14 (109.925, 106.750), U15 (115.513, 115.400), Y1 (105.500, 101.500), Y2 (109.350, 115.070) pin 1, board y down; U7 A1 corner (116.500, 103.250); U12 (114.000, 98.550).
If left alone
A regulator, the load switch, a buffer or an oscillator is reverse-powered at first power-up; U5, U6 and U10 would short their input rail to ground. A reversed U8 or U15 shorts VCCO_1V5 through the part, stresses U4's output and leaves the board unable to boot from flash or start the transceiver rails.
What to do
Keep 'confirm parts placement' on and, before production, check pin 1 of U5, U6, U8, U10, U11, U14, U15, Y1, Y2, U7 and U12 in JLC's placement preview against the pin-1 table; or get JLC's written rule for bottom-side angles and library offsets first.
Source
Frontend order files (CPL with library offsets on U1, U8, U11, U15, U17, U18; pin-1 table; JLC_ORDER_NOTES.txt); kicad-jlcpcb-tools fabrication.py, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Bouni/kicad-jlcpcb-tools/main/fabrication.py ; KiBot position notes, https://kibot.readthedocs.io/en/latest/notes_position.html ; JLCPCB pick-and-place help, https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pick-place-file-for-pcb-assembly

FE-63Minor

The order notes set the bottom stencil but not the top, where the 0201 parts and the five power modules also need a thin one.

The assembly remark asks for a 0.08 mm stencil on the bottom side (or a step-down at U7) and an X-ray of the boot flash U7. It says nothing about the top side, so JLC chooses there among its 0.10, 0.12 and 0.15 mm stencils; its own guide suggests 0.12 mm for boards that mix 0603 parts and 0.5 mm-pitch ICs, as this one does. How well paste releases is judged by the area ratio (opening area divided by its wall area), against the IPC-7525 guideline of 0.66 (JLC's stencil guide allows about 0.60 for electropolished and 0.55 for nano-coated stencils). At 0.12 mm the top-side 0201 resistors and capacitors come to 0.60 and the five MPM3833C modules (U2, U3, U4, U13, U16) to 0.61; at 0.10 mm every top-side part passes.

Where
Frontend top side: the 0201 parts (for example R1, R4, R5, R41, R55, R59) and the MPM3833C modules U2, U3, U4, U13, U16; order_frame_V3/JLC_ORDER_NOTES.txt, 'U7 solder paste' line.
If left alone
With a 0.12 mm top stencil the paste for the top-side 0201 parts and the power modules releases marginally, which raises the chance of starved joints.
What to do
Add 'top stencil 0.10 mm' to the assembly remark, or ask JLC which thickness it will use on the top side.
Source
Artix_Frontend_8L_V3.kicad_pcb paste layers and the paste Gerbers (Frontend_14L_V13_Test_Frame_V3_Gerbers.zip); Frontend order notes (order_frame_V3/JLC_ORDER_NOTES.txt); IPC-7525B stencil design guideline (area ratio 0.66)

FE-56Note

Y2's data sheet covers only a 15 pF load, so its drive into the 830 Ohm level-shift divider is not specified.

The 25 MHz oscillator Y2 drives R44 (150 Ohm) and R63 (680 Ohm) to ground to bring its 1.8 V swing down to about 1.47 V for the 1.5 V bank. The maker gives no output current or resistance; the divider draws about 2 mA. The FPGA still reads a high level if Y2's output resistance is below about 370 Ohm, which a normal CMOS output meets easily.

FE-17Note

On HDMI ports 1, 2, 7 and 8 the separate SYNC input floats, and the Frontend cannot tell which port it is on.

Every Frontend feeds cable pins 12/14 (CABLE_SYNC) into an LVDS input (B9/A9) with only a 100 Ohm resistor (R64) and no bias. The HDMI Extension Board drives these pins only on ports 3-6. On the other ports the input sees 0 V between its legs and can read either level or toggle on noise.

Where
Frontend J3.12/J3.14 -> U18 -> R64 -> U1 B9/A9 (LVDS_25, DIFF_TERM FALSE).
If left alone
Gateware that uses CABLE_SYNC on ports 1, 2, 7 or 8 sees random sync pulses.
What to do
Gateware must use the SYNC folded into the command line unless it knows it is on ports 3-6 (for example from a port number in the command stream).
Source
Frontend schematic V57 netlist and pin file; system datasheet V1, Frontend cable section; AMD DS181 LVDS input table, https://docs.amd.com/v/u/en-US/ds181_Artix_7_Data_Sheet

FE-35Note

Bank 14 runs at 1.5 V while bank 0 runs at 1.8 V during configuration; Vivado has not been asked whether it accepts this.

CFGBVS is tied to GND, so either 1.5 V or 1.8 V is allowed electrically. Bank 0 (CCLK) is on VCCAUX_1V8 and bank 14 (FCS_B, D00, D01) on VCCO_1V5; U8 translates to the 1.8 V flash. AMD's SPI table lists only matched bank voltages. The pin file sets CFGBVS GND and CONFIG_VOLTAGE 1.8.

Where
Frontend U1 E12 (CFGBVS) to GND; E10/R10 on VCCAUX_1V8; bank 14 on VCCO_1V5; U8 SN74AVC4T774.
If left alone
Probably no electrical problem; possibly a Vivado design-rule warning or error that needs to be understood.
What to do
